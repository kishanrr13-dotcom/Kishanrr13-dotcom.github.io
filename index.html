How to actually choose one

  1. Set a battery floor first. Anything under 4,500mAh will struggle to get most people through a full day of real use.
  2. Decide if you need the "Pro" camera. Extra lenses matter far less than sensor size and software processing — check reviews, not spec sheets.
  3. Check the software update promise. A cheaper phone with 4 years of updates can outlast a flagship that gets 2.
  4. Hold it before you buy it, if you can. Weight and one-hand reach vary more than photos suggest.
